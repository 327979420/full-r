What Is a Trading Rebate? How IBs, Forex Rebates and “Full Rebates” Work

Traders often hear the terms rebate, IB, referral link and full rebate. They are different parts of the same commercial relationship. Understanding where the money comes from makes it much easier to compare real trading costs.

The short version: a rebate is not extra trading profit. It is part or all of an eligible commission that an Introducing Broker returns to the trader to reduce trading costs that have already been incurred.

How do TMGM rebates work at Max Rebate?

A rebate returns an eligible part of trading-related commission, subject to account attribution, product rules and approval. Max Rebate is an independent service. Its published rebate table describes its own arrangement, not a rate guaranteed to every TMGM account.

Check the current published table, confirm eligibility, and compare the amount received with eligible trading records. A rebate does not reimburse every trading cost or guarantee a profit.

How do the trader, broker and IB relate to each other?

IB stands for Introducing Broker. In simple terms, an IB introduces clients to a broker and may help with account opening, account attribution and ongoing support. When eligible clients trade, the broker may pay the IB a commission under the relevant partnership arrangement.

The exact calculation differs by broker, account group and agreement, but the basic flow can be understood like this:

01Trader trades
02Broker earns spread / fees
03IB receives eligible commission
04IB returns a rebate

So a rebate is not money created from nowhere. It is the amount of eligible IB commission that the IB chooses to return to the trader.

Why do rebate providers offer different rates?

Once an IB receives commission, it can keep more of that commission as revenue or return more of it to the client.

That is why the same broker and account type can show different rebate figures on different rebate websites. The important question is not simply “who advertises the highest rebate?” but how much your exact account and product actually receives per standard lot.

Max Rebate focuses on reducing unnecessary intermediary deductions, publishing rebate structures clearly, and helping clients verify account type, product and rebate amount.

What does “full rebate” actually mean?

This term is easy to misunderstand. A full rebate does not mean that 100% of every spread and fee charged by the broker is refunded.

At Max Rebate, “full rebate” refers to returning the eligible IB commission available under the current arrangement to the client, subject to correct account attribution, valid trading activity and platform approval, rather than taking an additional service share from that eligible rebate.

Why does rebate transparency matter?

A trader should be able to answer at least four questions:

  1. Which account group am I in?
  2. What is the rebate per lot for the products I actually trade?
  3. Is my account correctly attributed to the relevant IB?
  4. Can I reconcile the rebate received with my own trading records?

This is why Max Rebate publishes information about TMGM rebates, TMGM account types, account-opening links, existing-account handling and trading costs. The goal is for clients to understand how commissions are calculated before they trade, rather than discovering missing value after settlement.

What should I check if a rebate does not match my estimate?

Match the account, instrument, eligible volume and reporting period before comparing amounts. Keep your own estimate, any pending amount shown in a statement, and money actually credited in separate records.

Rebates reduce costs — they are not a trading strategy

Trading more simply to earn more rebates is not sensible. More trades can also mean more spreads, commissions, slippage and market risk.

The better way to think about rebates is: if these trades would have happened anyway, the rebate can reduce part of their cost. For traders who already use EAs, systematic strategies or high-frequency approaches, small differences per lot naturally become more meaningful as legitimate trading volume grows.

Can an existing TMGM account receive rebates?

An existing account can be submitted for an eligibility review. A successful transfer or rebate activation cannot be promised in advance.

Follow the existing-account review steps, provide the information requested by the application form, and keep the reference number. If the account was registered more than 3 days ago, email Max Rebate and Max Rebate will explain how it can be handled. Never share passwords or verification codes, and do not use false details or another person's identity.

Frequently asked questions

Does a 100% rebate refund all trading costs?

No. At Max Rebate it refers to the eligible commission received and returnable under the arrangement. Spreads, fees and other trading costs are not automatically refunded in full.

Does submitting the form activate rebates?

No. Submission starts a review. Account attribution, eligibility and the applicable terms must be confirmed before rebates can be treated as active.

How can I check a rebate payment?

Compare the confirmed product rate and eligible standard lots with the amount recorded by the platform. Keep pending estimates separate from money received, and ask for a review if the records differ.

Why can a rebate differ from my estimate for the same trading volume?

Check that both records cover the same account, instrument and reporting period, using the confirmed account rate and eligible standard lots. Estimates, pending amounts (if shown) and actual credits are different states. Ask for a review if the records still differ; order count alone does not establish an underpayment.

What should I prepare before asking about a rebate difference?

Prepare your application reference, if available, the instrument, the reporting period and time zone, and the eligible volume, estimate and actual credit shown in the relevant records. Confirm the required supporting details through the existing contact channels. Do not publish full account records or share passwords or verification codes.
